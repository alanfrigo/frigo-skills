# Rig, retarget and ship

## Contract before changing a rig

Record rig version, rest/bind pose, parent hierarchy and bone names, scale and forward/up axes, intended display rate, and clip/action boundaries. Keep authoring controls (IK targets, poles, drivers, space switching, rig UI) separate from the deform skeleton the consumer accepts. Preserve a deliberate master/root versus hips distinction. Don't apply transforms to an existing skinned rig as a shortcut: compare evaluated bind, pose and imported orientation on a copy and correct the pipeline at the point where the spaces diverge.

- **Bind and deform:** Test neutral, deep bend, full extension, shoulder reach, twist of upper arm/forearm, planted ankle, and extreme pelvis motion. Check collapse, bulge, interpenetration, poles flipping, unweighted vertices and cross-side influence. Read the skin in silhouette and from several angles. Paint or normalize weights and limit influences to the target's budget; evaluate the post-limit version. Use twist bones or correctives only if the export target preserves them (or if the shot stays in Blender). Rigid parts can use one intended bone per piece, unlike organic joints.
- **FK/IK:** FK is a good baseline for free arcs; IK suits hand/sole contacts. Set stable pole planes and avoid straight-chain singularities and silent scale/stretch. Match evaluated transforms into the incoming control space, key the switch, then inspect the frames just before and after. On characters with facial controls, verify actual expressions/deformation, not channel names; test whether the target consumes bone, morph/shape-key or baked output.
- **Objects and creatures:** Define the actual support or hinge and range of motion rather than assuming a humanoid. Use observed gait and a contact schedule for each limb. Use constraints for mechanical joints or accessory attachments; verify if and how they are baked.

## Mocap and retargeting

1. Confirm the capture's rights, performers' permissions, source clock/frame rate, and length units. Import into a clean source scene; keep its action, scale and orientation unchanged as reference. Mark dropouts, contact intervals, root heading, floor level and reliable frames before smoothing.
2. Create an explicit source-to-target bone map including pelvis/root, extremities, and any unmapped or separately authored joints. Record rest pose offsets (e.g. A-pose vs T-pose) and bone roll/local axes. Compare a neutral pose and simple bend/twist tests; correct axis/handedness once, not per clip. Do not copy local Euler channels between unlike rests. Convert visual joint transforms through the source and target rest spaces or use an appropriate retargeter; scale translational root motion intentionally with proportions, not every limb transform.
3. Bake a target action on a copy; then clean jitter, occlusions, heading and contacts locally. Use hand or foot IK and adjust pelvis motion where limbs no longer reach. Keep good timing and meaningful asymmetry when filtering. If clips come from different systems, align their world direction, ground, contact phases and pacing before matching transitions. Compare the source, retarget and cleaned version at real speed.
4. Apply Williams' performance review: preserve or improve the story beats and support even when capture timing seems realistic. Re-key masked or weak reactions and ineffective passing poses; don't add blanket easing. Preserve the editable retarget and the final baked clip separately.

## Contacts, root motion and loops

- Place dedicated marker **heads** (or explicitly select tails) at the actual heel/toe/hand contact point, not at the ankle hinge. Put each interval on the stable pivot: a heel can move while the toe stays anchored. With Blender’s Z up, inspect floor height, penetration and clearance independently from drift. For moving platforms or props, judge in the surface's space; a world-fixed test will correctly report motion but that is not slip relative to the moving surface.
- Trace root travel, vertical motion, yaw and speed separately from hips' local sway. A world-moving clip should advance one measured stride per repeat with coherent speed and facing (or an intentional turn). For in-place clips, remove translation/yaw using the target policy, but keep believable support in the **composed** motion: world-space foot lock is measurable on the progressing source or with the gameplay trajectory applied, not on feet intended to sweep backwards through an in-place file.
- Decide whether the engine reads the armature object, a root bone track or a separate motion curve; don't apply displacement twice. Split clips with a reason: idle, cycle, start, stop, turn, land. Tag sync/contact events in the consuming format or a versioned sidecar if the interchange format does not carry them.
- Compare same-phase endpoints (and matching derivative/tangent behavior near the seam). Measure pose closure in root-relative space for traveling loops, and test root delta independently. Watch three or more repeats and at least one blend into and out of the clip. If frames 1 and 25 are a duplicate seam at 24 fps, duration between endpoints is one second. A 24-frame preview should not show the duplicated endpoint twice; don't inadvertently remove the terminal sample needed by the engine.

## Export and prove the handoff

1. Save source `.blend` and bake a copy for export; constrain to the intended action/range, frame rate and only needed nodes. Record naming and scaling, camera or preview spec and licensing. In Blender, use visual keying/sampling for constrained motions and keep the authoring file before clearing constraints. Confirm that rest pose, keys, root, shape keys and weights really travel through the chosen format.
2. Use GLB/glTF for compatible game/web targets, or the project's specified FBX/rig pipeline. Don't assume glTF exports control constraints, arbitrary DCC nodes, animation state machines or gameplay events. For Godot 4, import the GLB with animation enabled; inspect the Skeleton3D, AnimationPlayer clip names and lengths, trim/loop settings, and AnimationTree root-motion track if used. Preview imported skinning and play the clip in a small scene with ground and a recognizable forward marker. For Blender cinematics, retain controls, camera, cut handles and linked asset versions; test the render scene and write image sequences for recoverable delivery. Use an interchange format only for data the receiving application needs.
3. Validate at an actual gameplay/cinematic camera, full speed and frame-by-frame. Test state transitions, engine scale/facing, floor contact, seam, pose shape, compression/sampling artifacts, and expected events. Compare imported playback to the source playblast. A successfully parsed GLB by itself does not establish any of these.
4. Label playblasts with character/clip, version, framing, timecode or frame range, and FPS; use the same framing for A/B comparison. Cheap viewport playback or proxy sequences are appropriate for review. Keep final rendering parameters separate, and do not claim tests you only planned.
